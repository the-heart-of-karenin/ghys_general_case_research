Abstract
If G is a ﬁnitely generated group with generators {g1, . . . , gj} then an inﬁnite
order element f ∈ G is a distortion element of G provided lim inf

orientable surface and let Diﬀ( S)0 denote the identity component of the group
of C 1 diﬀeomorphisms of S. Our main result shows that if S has genus at

main result showing that certain groups, including a large c lass of higher rank
lattices, admit no homomorphisms to Diﬀ µ(S) with inﬁnite image.

Remark 1.2. It is straightforward to see that the property of being a distortio n ele-
ment is independent of the set of generators used to measure |f n|. It is also immediate
that if f is a distortion element then so is f k for all k > 0. The structure theorem for
ﬁnitely generated abelian groups implies that they have no distortion elements and
hence no abelian group contains any distortion elements.
It is easy to check (see Lemma 7.3) that central elements of the th ree dimensional
Heisenberg group are distortion elements. Lubotzky, Mozes and R aghunathan [16]
proved that irreducible non-uniform lattices in higher rank Lie group s have distor-
tion elements. (They prove the stronger result that there are ele ments in the group
whose word length has logarithmic growth; in this paper we use only th at the growth
is sublinear.) On the other hand, there are no distortion elements in b iautomatic
groups [12], mapping class groups [5] and the outer automorphism gr oup of a ﬁnitely
generated free group [1]. In particular, none of these groups hav e a subgroup that is
isomorphic to the three dimensional Heisenberg group.
Polterovich (1.6.C of [18]) proved that if S is a closed surface of genus at least
two and ω is a smooth measure on S, then ﬁnitely generated subgroups of Ham ω(S),
the group of Hamiltonian diﬀeomorphisms on S, do not contain distortion elements.
There is also a closely related result (Theorem 1.6.B of [18]) for ﬁnitely g enerated
subgroups of Diﬀ ω(S).
Our main theorem is a generalization of these results from [18]. We do n ot assume
that the invariant measure is smooth and in particular do not assume that its support
is all of S. In several respects there are parallels between properties of D iﬀ(S1) and
Diﬀ µ(S) for S a surface. One of the striking properties of homeomorphisms of S1 is
that periodic points all have the same period and if there are periodic points they
contain the support of any invariant measure. Of course, in gener al this is far from
true for surfaces. However, our main result shows this property does generalize for
distortion elements in Diﬀ µ(S).
Theorem 1.3. Suppose that S is a closed oriented surface, that f is a distortion
element in Diﬀ(S)0 and that µ is an f -invariant Borel probability measure.
1. If S has genus at least two then supp(µ) ⊂ Fix(f ).
2. If S = T 2 and Per(f ) ̸= ∅ then supp(µ) ⊂ Fix(f n), where n > 0 is the minimal
value for which Fix(f n) ̸= ∅.
3. If S = S2 and if f n has at least three ﬁxed points for some smallest n > 0, then
supp(µ) ⊂ Fix(f n).

This result is motivated by an interest in understanding the algebraic properties
of Diﬀ µ(S)0. We will apply it to obtain results showing that certain ﬁnitely generat ed
groups can only have homomorphic images in Diﬀ µ(S)0 which are ﬁnite.
Recall that a group G is called almost simple provided every normal subgroup
is ﬁnite or has ﬁnite index. Also G is called Kazhdan or said to satisfy property T
provided the identity is isolated in the unitary dual ˆG (see [15] or [20]).
Theorem 1.5. Suppose that S is a closed oriented surface of positive genus equipped
with a Borel probability measure µ and that G is a ﬁnitely generated group which is
almost simple and possesses a distortion element u. Suppose further that either µ
has inﬁnite support or that G is a Kazhdan group. Then any homomorphism φ :
G → Diﬀ µ(S) has ﬁnite image. The result is valid for S = S2 with the additional
assumption that φ(u) has at least three periodic points.
In the case S = S2 we may remove the rather artiﬁcial hypothesis on periodic
points of φ(u) at the expense of replacing the hypothesis of the existence of a d istortion
element with the stronger hypothesis that G contains a subgroup isomorphic to the
three-dimensional Heisenberg group. In this case the additional a ssumptions on µ are
not necessary.
Theorem 1.6. Suppose that S is a closed oriented surface equipped with a Borel
probability measure µ, and that G is a ﬁnitely generated almost simple group which
has a subgroup H isomorphic to the three-dimensional Heisenberg group. The n any
homomorphism φ : G → Diﬀ µ(S) has ﬁnite image.

lattice are suﬃcient to give the hypotheses of one of the results ab ove. We defer these
proofs to the last section.

3 Hyperbolic Geometry
Some of our proofs rely on mapping class group techniques that use hyperbolic geome-
try. In this section we establish notation and recall standard resu lts about hyperbolic
structures on surfaces.
Let S be a closed orientable surface. We will say that a connected open su bset
M of S has negative Euler characteristic if H1(M, R) is inﬁnite dimensional or if M
is of ﬁnite type and the usual deﬁnition of Euler characteristic has a negative value.
All such M have complete hyperbolic structures; further details can be foun d, for
example, in [10].
We use the Poincar´ e disk model for the hyperbolic plane H. In this model, H is
identiﬁed with the interior of the unit disk and geodesics are segment s of Euclidean
circles and straight lines that meet the boundary in right angles. A ch oice of hy-
perbolic structure on M provides an identiﬁcation of the universal cover ˜M of M
with H. Under this identiﬁcation covering translations become isometries o f H and
geodesics in M lift to geodesics in H. The compactiﬁcation of the interior of the unit
disk by the unit circle induces a compactiﬁcation of H by the ‘circle at inﬁnity’ S∞ .
Geodesics in H have unique endpoints on S∞ .
Each covering translation T extends to a homeomorphism (also called) T : H ∪
S∞ → H ∪ S∞ . The ﬁxed point set of a non-trivial T is either one or two points in
S∞ . We denote these point(s) by T + and T − , allowing the possibility that T + = T − .
If T + = T − , then T is said to be parabolic. If T + and T − are distinct, then T is said
to be hyperbolic and we may assume that T + is a sink and T − is a source. In this
case the geodesic connecting T − to T + is said to be the axis of T .
Suppose now that f ∈ Diﬀ(S) and that K ⊂ Fix(f ). Each component of S\Fix(f )
is f -invariant by [2]. If M is a component of S \K with negative Euler characteristic,
then we denote f |M by h to keep a clear distinction between f and f |M and to
simplify notation. We use the identiﬁcation of H with ˜M and write ˜h : H → H for
lifts of h : M → M to the universal cover. A fundamental result of Nielsen theory
is that every lift ˜h : H → H extends uniquely to a homeomorphism (also called)
˜h : H ∪ S∞ → H ∪ S∞ . (A proof of this fact appears in Proposition 3.1 of [14]). If
h : M → M is isotopic to the identity then there is a unique lift ˜h, called the identity
lift, that commutes with all covering translations and whose extension o ver S∞ is the
identity.
In certain contexts it is convenient to blow up isolated ends of M. More precisely,
we compactify an isolated end of M by adding a circle via the following well known
lemma, a proof of which can be found in Lemma 5.1 of [10]. This construc tion does
not depend on the hyperbolic structure of M and can be applied equally well when
M is topologically an annulus.

We need a few preliminary lemmas.
Lemma 7.1. Let G be a ﬁnitely generated almost simple group and let H be a normal
subgroup. If u ∈ H is a distortion element in G then it is also a distortion element
in H.
Proof. Since G is almost simple and H contains an inﬁnite order element, H has ﬁnite
index. Since H is a ﬁnite index subgroup of a ﬁnitely generated group, it is ﬁnitely
generated. Let {h1, . . . , hm} be a set of generators for H and let 1 = a0, a1, . . . , ak be
coset representatives of H. Deﬁne word length in H with respect to {h1, . . . , hm} and
word length in G with respect to the generating set {h1, . . . , hm, a1, . . . , ak}.
For each g ∈ G there exists 0 ≤ i ≤ k and h ∈ H such that g = aih. Choose
C such that |h|H ≤ C for the ﬁnitely many g satisfying |g|G ≤ 2. We claim that
|h|H ≤ C · |g|G for all g. The proof is by induction on |g|G with the |g|G ≤ 2 being
by construction. Given g ∈ G with l := |g|G ≥ 3, write g = ug1 where |u|G = 1
and |g1|G = l − 1. The inductive hypothesis implies that g1 = aih1 and uai = ajh2
where |h1|H ≤ C(l − 1) and |h2|H ≤ C. Setting h = h2h1 we have |h|H ≤ C ·l which
completes the inductive step.
It follows that |h|H ≤ C · |h|G for all h ∈ H which implies the lemma.
Lemma 7.2. Let G be a ﬁnitely generated almost simple group which contains a
distortion element and let H be a normal subgroup. Then any homomorphism ψ :
H → R is trivial.
Proof. Since G is almost simple, H is either ﬁnite or has ﬁnite index. Clearly the
result is true if H is ﬁnite, so we assume it has ﬁnite index. If u is a distortion
element in G then v := uk ∈ H for some k > 0. The normalizer D of v in G is inﬁnite
and hence has ﬁnite index in G; it is obviously contained in H. Thus D has ﬁnite
index in H. Since R contains neither torsion nor distortion elements, v, and hence D
is in the kernel of ψ for every homomorphism ψ : H → R. Since D has ﬁnite index
in H we conclude that ψ(H) is ﬁnite and hence trivial.
