Abstract.We prove that every codimension-one holomorphic Anosov dif-
feomorphism of a compact connected complex manifold is biholomorphi-
cally conjugate to a hyperbolic automorphism of a complex torus. This
verifies a conjecture of Ghys [Ghy95] in the codimension-one case and, in
particular, in complex dimension three.

Franks and Manning proved that an Anosov diffeomorphism on an infra-
nilmanifold is topologically conjugate to a hyperbolic affine automorphism
[Fra70, Man74]. The conjugacy and its inverse are H¨ older continuous, but
the conjugacy need not be aC 1 diffeomorphism in general: aC 1 conjugacy
identifies the eigenvalues of the corresponding derivative return maps at pe-
riodic points, whereas these eigenvalues can be changed by arbitrarily small
localC ∞ perturbations. A conjecture going back to Anosov and Smale as-
serts that every compact manifold supporting an Anosov diffeomorphism is
homeomorphic to an infra-nilmanifold [Sma67].

The dynamics of holomorphic diffeomorphisms on complex manifolds are
often more rigid than those of general differentiable maps, owing to the com-
patibility between the dynamics and the complex structure. In [Ghy95], Ghys
proposed the following conjecture.
Every holomorphic Anosov diffeomorphism is biholomor-
phically conjugate to an affine automorphism of a complex
infra-nilmanifold.
Ghys verified the conjecture for complex surfaces [Ghy95, Theorem A]. Can-
tat [Can04, Theorem 1.4] proved the corresponding classification for projective
manifolds, assuming either that both invariant distributions are holomorphic or
that the diffeomorphism has complex codimension one. Zhang [Zha26] proved
a regularity bootstrap and algebraic classification for holomorphic Anosov dif-
feomorphisms that are bi-Lipschitz conjugate to affine models.
In higher dimensions, the conjecture remains open in general. In this note,
we prove it for holomorphic Anosov diffeomorphisms of complex codimension
one, meaning that dim C Es = 1 or dim C Eu = 1.
TheoremA.Letf:X→Xbe a codimension-one holomorphic Anosov dif-
feomorphism of a compact complex manifold. ThenXis biholomorphic to a
complex torus, andfis biholomorphically conjugate to a hyperbolic holomor-
phic torus automorphism.
In particular, Theorem A verifies Ghys’s conjecture for compact complex
threefolds: since both invariant distributions are nonzero, one of them must
have complex dimension one.
1.1.Strategy of the proof.In this subsection, letf:X→Xbe a
codimension-one holomorphic Anosov diffeomorphism of a compact complex
manifold of complex dimensiond. Replacingfbyf −1 if necessary, we assume
that dimC Es = 1.
Topological conjugacy.The first step is to establish the topological conjugacy.
Theorem 1.1.Letf:X→Xbe a holomorphic Anosov diffeomorphism of
a compact complex manifold of complex dimensiond≥2, withdim C Es =
1. ThenXis homeomorphic toT 2d, andfis topologically conjugate to a
hyperbolic toral automorphism.
The cased= 2 is covered by [Ghy95, Theorem A]. Ford≥3, Ghys [Ghy95,
Theorem B] proved this statement under the additional assumption of topo-
logical transitivity. The transitivity assumption is only used to show that the
unstable holonomy maps are globally defined on every stable leaf. In this pa-
per, we establish the global existence of the unstable holonomy by using a
specific geometrically defined non-stationary linearization of the stable leaves,
with respect to which the unstable holonomy maps are linear and thus glob-
ally defined. This enables us to remove the topological transitivity assumption
from Ghys’s Theorem B.

We note that Theorem 1.1 does not follow from the Franks–Newhouse clas-
sification of codimension-one Anosov diffeomorphisms [Fra70,New70], since in
our settingE s andE u have real dimension at least two.
Holomorphic conjugacy.The second step is to improve the regularity of the
conjugacy. A difficulty is that a complex manifold homeomorphic toT 2d need
not be a complex torus whend≥3: real tori admit nonstandard complex
structures; see [Cat02,COP10]. 1 This leads to the following question.
Can a nonstandard complex structure onT 2d,d≥3, admit a
holomorphic Anosov diffeomorphism?
We expect the answer to be negative. Theorem A proves this in complex
dimension three and, more generally, in the codimension-one case. By contrast,
Xu and Zhang [XZ25] constructed holomorphic partially hyperbolic systems
on real tori with nonstandard complex structures in complex dimensions at
least five.
The following theorem gives a negative answer to the question above under
the additional assumption thatE s andE u are holomorphic.
Theorem 1.2.Letf:X→Xbe a holomorphic Anosov diffeomorphism of
a compact complex manifold. Assume thatXis homeomorphic to a torus
and that the invariant distributionsE s andE u are holomorphic. ThenXis
biholomorphic to a complex torus, andfis biholomorphically conjugate to a
holomorphic torus automorphism.
The main idea is to decompose deck transformations of the universal cover
into their stable and unstable components and use the holomorphicity of both
foliations to show that these components are biholomorphic. Their closure
gives a transitive action by holomorphic translations, forcing the complex struc-
ture to be translation-invariant.
To apply Theorem 1.2, we establish the holomorphicity of the invariant
distributions.
Theorem 1.3.Letf:X→Xbe a holomorphic Anosov diffeomorphism of
a compact complex manifold withdim C Es = 1. Then bothE s andE u are
holomorphic subbundles ofT 1,0X.
The invariant distributionsE s andE u are a priori H¨ older continu-
ous [HPS77]. In the setting of Theorem 1.3, Ghys [Ghy95, Proposition 2.2]
proved thatE u is holomorphic by estimating the quasiconformal distortion
of the unstable holonomy maps. This method does not extend directly to
Es since holomorphicity and conformality are no longer equivalent in higher
complex dimensions. As an alternative, we prove the holomorphicity ofE s
directly by anL 2-estimate for the antiholomorphic derivatives of suitable
smooth approximations.
Theorem A then follows from Theorems 1.1, 1.2, and 1.3.

1.2.Obstructions in higher codimension.One may ask whether the strat-
egy of the proof of Theorem A extends to higher codimension. The three steps
of the proof encounter different obstructions.
The topological conclusion of Theorem 1.1 does not extend to higher codi-
mension in general. Cantat constructed a holomorphic Anosov diffeomorphism
on a compact complex fourfold that is not homeomorphic to a torus, with
dimC Es = dim C Eu = 2; see [Can04, Example 1.5]. Section 5 gives another
example on a non-torus nilmanifold. Thus, a general classification must allow
algebraic models beyond tori.
Theorem 1.2 itself does not require the codimension-one assumption. Its
proof, however, uses the additive structure of the toral model and the density
of the stable and unstable projections of the lattice to construct a transi-
tive group of holomorphic translations. This argument does not extend di-
rectly to general infra-nilmanifolds. It is natural to ask whether an analogous
classification by affine automorphisms holds for manifolds homeomorphic to
infra-nilmanifolds, under the assumption that both invariant distributions are
holomorphic. However, even if such a generalization holds, the holomorphicity
assumption remains an obstruction to applying it in full generality.
Indeed, Section 5 constructs a holomorphic Anosov automorphism of a com-
pact nilmanifold with a left-invariant complex structure whose stable and un-
stable distributions are both smooth but non-holomorphic. Thus, the regu-
larity conclusion of Theorem 1.3 fails in higher codimension. This example
is already an algebraic model consistent with Ghys’s conjecture, showing that
the conjectured classification must accommodate non-holomorphic invariant
distributions.
1.3.Structure of the paper.Section 2 proves Theorem 1.1, Section 3 proves
Theorem 1.2, and Section 4 proves Theorem 1.3. Section 5 constructs a holo-
morphic Anosov automorphism whose invariant distributions are both non-
holomorphic.

3.Proof of Theorem 1.2
The proof rests on three observations. First, all deck transformations of
the universal cover eXare biholomorphic. Second, each deck translation can
be decomposed into stable and unstable components. When the stable and
unstable foliations are holomorphic, both components act biholomorphically,
and their translation vectors form dense subgroups of the corresponding linear
subspaces. Third, locally uniform limits of holomorphic maps are holomorphic.
Taking limits therefore shows that all translations act biholomorphically, which
