TEICHMÜLLER AND MODULI SPACES FOR COMPLEX
NILMANIFOLDS
KONSTANTIN WEHLER
Abstract. We develop several tools to study the local and global structure of the
Teichmüller and moduli spaces for complex nilmanifolds. As a starting point, we
prove a structure theorem for holomorphic maps between complex nilmanifolds,
which allows for an explicit description of the Teichmüller and moduli spaces. We
then introduce two period maps, and give general criteria for the existence of a
complex structure on these spaces.
To illustrate the theory, we prove a global Torelli theorem for various classes of
nilmanifolds, including principal torus bundles over tori and almost abelian nil-
manifolds. In particular, we show that there are nilmanifolds of arbitrary dimen-
sion and nilpotency index for which the Teichmüller space admits the structure
of a complex manifold.

Teichmüller and moduli spaces.Let M = Γ\G be an even-dimensional nilman-
ifold and let g be the Lie algebra of G. By left-translations, any almost complex
structure J : g → g extends to an almost complex structure onG. This almost com-
plex structure is invariant under the action of the latticeΓ and thus descends to
an almost complex structure onM, also denoted byJ. If J is integrable, then the
compact complex manifold(M, J) is called a complex nilmanifold.
The lattice Γ and the Lie algebrag determine the Lie groupG up to canonical
isomorphism, so we will often omit the Lie groupG from the notation and just say
M is a nilmanifold of type(g, Γ). The main objects of our study are the following
spaces: we define
M(g, Γ) = {complex nilmanifolds (M, J)} / ∼=
as the moduli space of biholomorphism classes of complex nilmanifolds of type(g, Γ).
The Teichmüller space
T (g) = {complex nilmanifolds (M, J)} / ∼
is defined by the equivalence relation∼ identifying two complex nilmanifolds if they
are biholomorphic via a diffeomorphism of M isotopic to the identity. Following
the results of Section 2, we will see that the structure of the Teichmüller space only
depends on the underlying Lie algebra, so we omit the lattice from the notation. Our
main goal is to identify pairs(g, Γ) for which M(g, Γ) or T (g) can be endowed, in a
natural way, with the structure of a complex space.
In the same way as above, one can also define the moduli and Teichmüller spaces
of all possible complex structures on M. However, it was shown in [Cat04] that
even for the real six-dimensional torus these spaces have countably many connected
components of unbounded dimension, so we will restrict our considerations to left-

formation of a complex nilmanifold is again a complex nilmanifold [Rol09b; HST26].
Under additional assumptions the spaceT (g) even makes up a union of connected
components in the Teichmüller space of all complex structures onM [Rol09a].

Holomorphic maps between complex nilmanifolds.Since the spacesM(g, Γ)
and T (g) are defined by the existence of certain holomorphic maps between complex
nilmanifolds, our first goal will be to provide a classification of such maps. It is a
classical result that a holomorphic map f : Γ\Cn → Λ\Cm between complex tori
lifts to an affine mapF : Cn → Cm. Concretely, there is a linear mapΦ: Cn → Cm
with Φ(Γ) ⊂ Λ and an element λ ∈ Λ such that F = Φ + λ. The following result
generalises this fact to holomorphic maps between arbitrary complex nilmanifolds.
Theorem A(Theorem 2.1) —Let f : X → Y be a holomorphic map between com-
plex nilmanifolds X = (Γ\G, J) and Y = (Λ\H, I). Then there is a holomorphic lift
F : (G, J) → (H, I) of f such that F = Rh ◦ Φ, where Rh is a right-translation onH
and Φ: G → H is a group homomorphism withΦ(Γ) ⊂ Λ.
As a consequence of Theorem A, we have a much simpler description of the Teich-
müller and moduli spaces. Let us denote by
C(g) = {J : g → g | J2 = − id, J integrable}
the space of complex structures ong; for any latticeΓ the space C(g) contains all
left-invariant complex structures on a nilmanifold of type(g, Γ). The space C(g)
can be embedded as a locally closed subspace of a complex Grassmannian and,
contrary to the Teichmüller and moduli spaces, it always comes equipped with the
structure of a (possibly singular) complex space. The groupG acts onC(g) by right-
translations and since every automorphism of the latticeΓ ⊂ G extends uniquely to
an automorphism of the Lie groupG [VGS00], we have an action ofAut(Γ) on C(g),
which descends to an action on the quotientC(g)/G.
Corollary B(Corollary 4.3) —For any latticeΓ ⊂ G we have T (g) = C(g)/G and
M(g, Γ) = T (g)/ Aut(Γ).
The space C(g) also admits an action by the automorphism groupAut(g), and
the quotientC(g)/ Aut(g) has already been studied for several classes of Lie algebras
[Ceb+16; LUV23; And+26]. While understanding the space C(g)/ Aut(g) is of in-
terest in its own right, it does not parametrise complex structures on any nilmanifold
since the elements ofAut(g) usually do not descend to the quotientΓ\G. As already
illustrated by the moduli space of Kodaira surfaces [Bor84], the situation becomes
much more complicated as soon as the lattice starts to play a role.
Unfortunately, the simpler description of the Teichmüller and moduli spaces given
in Corollary B certainly does not imply that they have any nice geometric structure.
In Section 4 we will give a precise meaning to the question whether or notT (g) or
M(g, Γ) admits a natural complex structure. In this case, we will just say thatT (g),
respectively M(g, Γ), exists as a complex space or a complex manifold.
We will see that one of the main obstructions for these spaces to admit a com-
plex structure is the existence of too many automorphisms. More precisely, to any
complex nilmanifold parametrised byC(g) one can attach the dimension of its auto-
morphism group, that is, the group of its biholomorphisms. We will show that if this
dimension is not constant on the connected components ofC(g), then the Teichmüller
space cannot exist as a complex space. For example, this occurs if the Lie algebrag
is non-abelian and admits a complex parallelisable structure. In fact, bothT (g) and
M(g, Γ) are far from Hausdorff in this case (see Proposition 7.1).
Period maps. One of the most important geometric features of complex nilmani-
folds is that their canonical bundle is always trivial [BDV09; Sal01]. While complex

tori are the only nilmanifolds that admit a Kähler metric, the Kähler case might give
some insight into what one could hope for when considering deformations of complex
nilmanifolds.
Two central results in the deformation theory of Calabi–Yau manifolds, i.e., Kähler
manifolds with trivial canonical bundle, are the Tian–Todorov theorem and the local
Torelli theorem. The former states that for a Calabi–Yau manifoldY, the Kuranishi
space Kur(Y )isalwayssmooth. Moreover, sinceeverysmalldeformationofaCalabi–
Yau manifold is again a Calabi–Yau manifold one can define a local period map on
Kur(Y ) by mapping a pointt ∈ Kur(Y ) to the de Rham class of a trivialising section
of the canonical bundle ofYt. The local Torelli theorem states that this map is always
a holomorphic embedding.
Unfortunately, both of these statements are no longer true for complex nilmani-
folds. As a consequence of a result by Rollenske [Rol09b], it was recently shown that
the Kuranishi family of a complex nilmanifold only contains complex nilmanifolds
[HST26]. However, the Kuranishi space of a complex nilmanifold is not necessarily
smooth. For instance, it is almost always singular for complex parallelisable nil-
manifolds [Rol11b; PRW26]. Secondly, and perhaps more surprisingly, we will see
that in a holomorphic family of complex nilmanifolds there can be infinitesimal dir-
ections which no longer have trivial canonical bundle. Due to this phenomenon a
holomorphic local period map can only be defined on a suitable reduction of the
Kuranishi space of a complex nilmanifold which, unlike in the Kähler case, is not
necessarily injective. Nevertheless, it turns out that such a local period map is an
important tool in the study of the Teichmüller space. For example, based on a result
of Catanese [Cat13] we obtain the following useful criterion.
PropositionC (Proposition5.1, Lemma5.6) — Let X be ann-dimensional complex
nilmanifold with underlying Lie algebrag. If every left-invariant d-exact (n − 1, 1)-
form is ∂-exact, then the natural map
Kur(X) → T (g)
is a local homeomorphism.
Furthermore, since we consider complex nilmanifolds with a fixed underlying Lie
algebra g, wecanextendthelocalperiodmaptoamaponthespace C(g). Concretely,
if M is a nilmanifold of dimension2n with underlying Lie algebra g, then, up to
scaling, the canonical bundle of a complex nilmanifold(M, J) can be trivialised by
a unique closed left-invariant(n, 0)-form. So we obtain a map C(g) → PH n(g, C)
by sending a complex structureJ ∈ C (g) to the class of an associated(n, 0)-form
on (M, J). It turns out that this map is holomorphic on a reduction of the complex
space C(g), and it descends to a map
P : T (g) → PH n(g, C).
We callP the global period map. The image of this map is contained in the so-called
period domain D, which is an open subset of a projective variety inPH n(g, C). One
of our main goals is to understand whenP is injective, as this has strong implications
for the structure of the Teichmüller space.

Roughly speaking, a principal torus bundle series for a left-invariant complex
structure J on M = Γ\G is a filtration on the Lie algebrag of G compatible with
both the complex structureJ and the latticeΓ (see Definition 1.4). Geometrically,
such a filtration induces a tower of principal bundles as above such that the complex
structure J induces a complex structure on every nilmanifoldMi and the maps πi
are holomorphic with respect to these complex structures.
In [Rol09a] Rollenske proved the existence of a principal torus bundle series for
several classes of complex nilmanifolds. Furthermore, he showed that such a torus
bundle series is often stable in the sense that it is compatible with all complex
structures on a given Lie algebrag and also with every lattice. As a first application
we study the Teichmüller spaces for principal torus bundles over a torus.

2n with a stable principal torus bundle series of length2. If dim Aut(X) = 1 for

is injective.

We will see several examples of nilmanifolds which satisfy the assumptions of this
theorem, among them is the class of Kodaira manifolds. Unfortunately, if one loosens
any of the assumptions in Theorem D, then it only holds for special classes of Lie
algebras. One of these classes are Lie algebras whose commutatorC1g is at most
two-dimensional, in particular, they are at most 3-step nilpotent. In [Rol09a] Rol-

commutator that admit a stable torus bundle series. Based on his classification we
obtain a fairly complete picture for this class.
Theorem E (Theorem 8.1) —Let g be a nilpotent Lie algebra admitting complex
structures. If dim C1g ≤ 2, then we have the following cases.
(i) If dim C1g = 1, then T (g) exists as a complex manifold and the global period
map P is an embedding.
(ii) If g is 2-step nilpotent and the centreZg is two-dimensional, thenT (g) does
not exist as a complex space in general, butP is generically injective on the
connected components ofT (g) containing complex parallelisable structures.
(iii) If g is 2-step nilpotent and dim Zg is odd, then T (g) does not exist as a
complex space in general.
(iv) If g is 3-step nilpotent, thenT (g) exists as a complex manifold and the global
period map P is an embedding.

Another class for which the existence of a stable principal torus bundle seriesS has
recently been proved is the class of so-called almost abelian Lie algebras [And+26].
An almost abelian Lie algebra is a non-abelian Lie algebra admitting a codimen-

abelian Lie algebras admitting a complex structure [Arr+25]. Even though neither
the nilpotency index nor the dimension of the commutator is bounded in this class,
it turns out that almost abelian Lie algebras are very well suited for the study of our

Theorem F (Theorem 9.4, Proposition 9.8) —Let g be a nilpotent almost abelian
Lie algebra admitting complex structures.
(i) The Teichmüller space T (g) exists as a complex manifold and the period
map P : T (g) → D is an embedding.
(ii) The period mapP S : T (g) → U is injective if and only ifg is the real Lie
algebra underlying a Kodaira surface.
Finally, we apply the previous results to study the Teichmüller and moduli spaces
for nilmanifolds of dimension six. It was shown by Salamon in [Sal01] that there are
18 isomorphism classes of six-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebras admitting a complex
structure. While a complete description of all Teichmüller and moduli spaces in this
class requires a more detailed analysis, we already cover some parts as an immediate
consequence of our previous results. For instance, we obtain
Theorem G (Theorem 10.1) —Let g be a nilpotent Lie algebra of dimension six
admitting complex structures. If dim C1g ≤ 2, then P : T (g) → D ⊂ PH3(g, C)
is injective and T (g) exists as a complex manifold, unlessg is the real Lie algebra
underlying the Iwasawa manifold in which caseT (g) is non-Hausdorff.
A different approach to understanding the Teichmüller and moduli spaces, which
avoids the problem of dealing with non-Hausdorff spaces, was taken by Meersseman
in [Mee19]. Instead of considering the Teichmüller and moduli spaces as topological
spaces, Meersseman constructed them as stacks for an arbitrary smooth compact
manifold. Moreover, he showed that under a mild condition on the dimension of the
automorphism group, these stacks are analytic [Mee19], which is far from true on
the level of topological spaces. Although the Teichmüller and moduli stacks are in
general difficult to describe, our results suggest that an explicit description of some
connected components should be possible for nilmanifolds admitting left-invariant
complex structures.

1.2. Complex nilmanifolds. Let g be a nilpotent Lie algebra and let G be an
associated simply connected nilpotent Lie group. A latticeΓ ⊂ G is a discrete co-
compact subgroup ofG. By the results of [Mal51] a lattice exists if and only if there
is a rational subalgebragQ of g such that gQ ⊗ R = g. Such a subalgebra is called a
rational structure forg and its existence is equivalent to the existence of a basis of
g∗ with rational structure constants.
If Gdoes admitalattice Γ, thenΓactson Gby multiplicationfromthe left andthe
compact manifoldM = Γ\G is called anilmanifold. Every complex structureJ on g
induces a complex structure onG, making (G, J) into a complex manifold on which
all left-translations are holomorphic. Since the latticeΓ acts by left-multiplication,
this left-invariant complex structure onG descends to a complex structure onM.
A complex structure onM is said to be left-invariant if it is induced by a complex
structure on g in this way.
Definition 1.2— Acomplex nilmanifold is a complex manifold(M, J), where M
is a nilmanifold andJ is a left-invariant complex structure.
Remark 1.3 — It was shown by Hasegawa in [Has89] that a nilmanifold is formal if
and only if it is diffeomorphic to a torus. In particular, a complex nilmanifoldX
does not carry a Kähler metric or satisfy the∂∂-lemma unlessX is a complex torus.

2. Holomorphic maps between complex nilmanifolds
The goal of this section is to prove the following structure theorem for holomorphic
maps between complex nilmanifolds.
Theorem 2.1— Let f : X → Y be a holomorphic map between complex nilmanifolds
X = (Γ \G, J) and Y = (Λ \H, I). Then there is a holomorphic lift F : (G, J) →

parallelisable nilmanifolds and later proved by Winkelmann for holomorphic maps
between arbitrary complex parallelisable nilmanifolds [Win98, Theorem 5.2.5].

4. Teichmüller and moduli spaces
In this section, we formulate the moduli problems we aim to solve. We start by
introducing the main objects of our study: the Teichmüller and moduli space of
complex nilmanifolds of a given type. We also recall the notions of coarse and fine
moduli spaces in our setting and discuss some existence criteria.
4.1. Definitions. Let M be a smooth compact connected manifold admitting com-
plex structures. We denote byDiff(M) the group of diffeomorphisms ofM and by
Diff0(M) the connected component of the identity inDiff(M), that is, the group of
diffeomorphisms of M smoothly isotopic to the identity. There is an action of these
groups on the space
C(M) = {J ∈ End(T M) | J2 = − id, J integrable}
of complex structures onM given by
(4.1) (f, J) 7→ f∗J = d f−1 ◦ J ◦ d f.
The so-calledTeichmüller spaceof M is defined as the quotient
T (M) = C(M)/ Diff0(M)
and themoduli spaceof complex structures onM is defined as
M(M) = C(M)/ Diff(M).
Alternatively, one can defineM(M) as the quotient of the Teichmüller space by the
mapping class groupDiff(M)/ Diff0(M). We consider both the Teichmüller and the
moduli space as topological spaces equipped with the quotient topology ofC(M).
The connected components of the Teichmüller space are given by the deformation
classes in the large. Concretely, two complex structuresJ and J ′ on M correspond
to points in the same connected component of T (M) if and only if the complex
manifold (M, J) is a deformation of (M, J ′) in the large [Cat13, Corollary 6]. In
general, it is a difficult task to identify connected components ofT (M) and many
examples where this is known to be possible belong to the class of nilmanifolds
[Cat04; Rol09a; And+26].
From now onM = Γ\G will denote a nilmanifold with underlying Lie algebrag
and we will considerC(g) ⊂ C (M) as the space of left-invariant complex structures
on M. We would like to restrict the notions of the Teichmüller and moduli space
to the space C(g). However, the action (4.1) does not preserve C(g), that is, for
an arbitrary diffeomorphism f ∈ Diff(M) and a complex structure J ∈ C (g) the
complex structure f∗J is not necessarily left-invariant. We define the Teichmüller
space of complex nilmanifolds of type(g, Γ) as

5. Period maps
In this section we introduce two period maps. These will be our main tools for
the study of the Teichmüller space.
We first explain how the classical period map defined for families of Calabi–Yau
manifolds can also be defined as a holomorphic map on a suitable reduction of the
space of left-invariant complex structures on a given nilmanifold or holomorphic
families thereof. We then investigate how the behaviour of this map corresponds to
the structure of the Teichmüller space.
Next, we introduce our second period map which depends on the choice of a stable
principal torus bundle series on a given Lie algebra. This map is roughly defined
by mapping a given complex nilmanifold with the structure of an iterated principal
torus bundle to the product of complex tori appearing as the fibres of this bundle.
We then study some basic properties of this map and explain how the two period
maps are related.

introduction, the Tian–Todorov theorem (see [Tia87]) states that a Calabi–Yau

10. Nilmanifolds of dimension six
In this section, we will apply our previous results to the the class of six-dimensional
nilmanifolds. Here, we will only consider those nilmanifolds for which the descrip-
tion of the Teichmüller or moduli space follows directly from one of the preceding
theorems. The remaining cases will be treated in a separate article.
Following Salamon’s notation [Sal01], we describe a nilpotent Lie algebrag, or
rather its isomorphism class, by writing g = ( de1, de2, . . . , de2n) for a given basis
e1, . . . , e2n of g∗, and abbreviating eij = ei ∧ ej to ij. For example, the tuple
(0, 0, 0, 12) represents the four-dimensional Lie algebra with the single non-trivial
differential de4 = e12.
Real nilpotent Lie algebras are classified up to dimension seven, which is the
smallest dimension where infinite families start to appear (see for instance [Mag86]).
The analysis which Lie algebras up to dimension six admit a complex structure was
carried out by Salamon in [Sal01]: up to dimension four there are two non-abelian
nilpotent Lie algebras, namely,(0, 0, 0, 12) and (0, 0, 12, 13). The former is the real
Lie algebra underlying a Kodaira surface and the latter does not admit a complex
structure [Sal01, Proposition 2.3].
In dimension six the situation becomes more involved. There are 34 isomorphism
classes of real nilpotent Lie algebras and Salamon’s classification states that a six-
dimensional nilpotent Lie algebra g admits a complex structure if and only ifg is
isomorphic to one of the following Lie algebras (cf. [Sal01, Table A.1] or [Uga07,
Theorem 8]):
h1 = (0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0)
