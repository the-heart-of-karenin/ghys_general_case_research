BIHOLOMORPHISM TYPE OF LEFT-INV ARIANT COMPLEX
STRUCTURES ON NILPOTENT LIE GROUPS
KEIZO HASEGAWA, SÖNKE ROLLENSKE, LORENZO SILLARI, ADRIANO TOMASSINI
AND KONSTANTIN WEHLER
Abstract.In this note we prove a conjecture by Hasegawa stating that a simply
connected, nilpotent Lie group of dimension2nendowed with a left-invariant
complex structure is biholomorphic toCn.
1.Introduction
The uniformisation theorem is a cornerstone of the theory of complex manifolds.
It characterises the topological, algebraic-geometric and differential-geometric prop-
erties of a Riemann surface in terms of its universal cover being isomorphic toP1,C
orH. The problem of characterising manifolds with a fixed type of universal cover
or determining properties of the universal cover of a certain type of manifold has
continued to see interest ever since, and gained traction especially in Kähler and
algebraic geometry over the last decades, see for instance [Kol95, CDS14, GKT18].
In the case of non-Kähler manifolds the picture is, as usual, less systematic and
focuses on the homogeneous case. Here, the most prominent conjecture has been
proposed by Hasegawa [Has09, Has10].
Conjecture 1.1—LetGbe a simply connected, nilpotent Lie group of dimension
2nand letJbe a left-invariant complex structure onG. Then the complex manifold
(G, J)is biholomorphic toC n.
We prove the conjecture by explicitly constructing a biholomorphism, which turns
out to be polynomial in exponential coordinates.
Theorem 1.2—LetGbe a simply connected, nilpotent Lie group of dimension2n
and letJbe a left-invariant complex structure onG. Then the complex manifold
(G, J)is biholomorphic toC n by a polynomial map. In particular, the universal
cover of a complex nilmanifold of dimensionnis biholomorphic toCn.
This theorem was already known in several special cases: Nakamura addressed
the case of complex nilpotent Lie groups [Nak75], while the surface case was impli-
citly contained in Kodaira’s work [Kod66], and explicitly in the work of Oeljeklaus–
Richthofer [OR84].

with left-invariant complex structure, provide a rich source of examples of compact

Kodaira–Thurston manifold [Thu76].
In this context, so-called nilpotent complex structures have been introduced in
[CFGU97], and recently Kanda [Kan26] gave an explicit constructive proof of The-
orem 1.2 in this special case. In terms of the universal cover, nilpotent complex
structures can be characterised by(G, J)being an iterated holomorphic principal
bundle for the additive groupC, so that this case can also be abstractly deduced
from the Oka–Grauert principle [Gra58].
In Example 4.1 we explicitly compute a polynomial biholomorphism from a6-
dimensional Lie group with a non-nilpotent complex structure toC3, exhibiting the
generality of our result.
The key ingredient for the general case, beyond the classical work of Snow [Sno85,
Sno86], is a theorem by Bialynicki-Birula and Rosenlicht [BBR62] stating that an
injective polynomial self-map ofR n is already surjective. Surjectivity of injective
algebraic self-maps was first established by Newman for polynomials inR2 [New60],
and by Bialynicki-Birula and Rosenlicht for polynomial self-maps ofR n and for
regular self-maps of complex algebraic varieties [BBR62]. The complex case was also
obtained independently by Ax, Grothendieck and Borel [Ax69, Gro66, Bor69].
As a direct consequence of the biholomorphism constructed in the proof of The-
orem 1.2 being polynomial, the applications obtained by Kanda [Kan26] under the
nilpotency assumption onJextend to arbitrary left-invariant complex structures. A
further application to the moduli of complex nilmanifolds is developed in the thesis
of Wehler [Weh26].
One should note that the analogue of Theorem 1.2 fails if the Lie groupGis no
longer nilpotent, but merely solvable. This can already be observed for the class
of two-dimensional complex solvmanifolds, known as Inoue surfaces (cf. [Has05]),
whose universal cover is biholomorphic toC×H. Nonetheless, Hasegawa [Has10]
conjectured that, forGa simply connected, unimodular, solvable Lie group with a
left-invariant complex structureJ, the complex manifold(G, J)is always Stein.

2.Preliminaries and notation
LetGbe a simply connected, nilpotent Lie group of dimension2nwith associated
Lie algebrag. A left-invariant almost complex structure onGis an endomorphism
J:g→gwithJ 2 =−id, extended to all ofT Gby left translations. The almost
complex structureJyields a decomposition of the complexified Lie algebra
gC =g 1,0 ⊕g 0,1

4.Concluding Remarks
If the complex structureJis nilpotent in the sense of Cordero–Fernández–Gray–
Ugarte [CFGU97], then there exists a basisZ1, . . . , Zn ofg 1,0 compatible with the
ascending central series ofg. In this case, it follows directly from the argument in the
previous section that the degree of the polynomials given by the mapΦis bounded
by the nilpotency index of the Lie algebrag. This is explicitly demonstrated by the
examples given in [Kan26, Section 5]. However, as the following example shows, this
bound no longer holds if the complex structure is non-nilpotent.
Example 4.1— The biholomorphismΦgiven in the proof of Theorem 1.2 is
polynomial foreveryleft-invariant complex structure, including those that are non-
nilpotent. In real dimension6there are, up to isomorphism, exactly two nilpotent
